Faults

Faults are what synth does to your system while your drivers run: it freezes and kills nodes and pods, cuts and slows the network, caps CPU and, if you opt in, moves clocks. This page explains how to choose which faults run, how synth plans them from the seed, and how to pause or script them.

The fault catalog

Section titled “The fault catalog”

synth has twelve fault kinds. Ten are on by default; the two clock kinds are opt-in. Run synth kinds to list them.

Kind What it does
node.pause Freezes a node container. It stays on the network but answers nothing.
node.kill, node.stop Kills or gracefully stops a node container, and starts it again after the fault’s duration. Every pod on it dies.
node.throttle Caps a node’s CPU to a fraction of a core.
node.partition Drops traffic between groups of nodes, optionally in one direction only.
node.netem Adds latency, jitter and packet loss on a node’s interface.
pod.kill Force-deletes a pod; its controller recreates it.
pod.pause Stops every process in a pod with SIGSTOP until the fault ends.
pod.partition, pod.netem The network faults above, inside one pod’s network namespace.
pod.clock, pod.clock_all Shift the clocks of one pod, or of every fault-target pod at once. Opt-in.

Mechanisms, parameters and the matching Antithesis fault names are in Fault kinds.

Configure faults

Section titled “Configure faults”

The faults: section of synthesis.yaml controls the fault injector:

Key Default Meaning
enabled [all] Fault kinds to use. all, node.* and pod.* expand to every default kind of that group. Clock kinds must be named exactly.
nodes agents Which k3d nodes node faults may target: agents, or all to add servers other than server-0.
podSelector synthesis.dev/fault-target=true Label selector for pods that pod faults may target.
intensity 0.15 Mean planned faults started per second of driver phase. An explicit 0 turns planned faults off.
minDuration, maxDuration 2s, 15s Bounds of each fault’s duration.
maxNodeFaults 1 How many nodes or pods may be disrupted (paused, killed or stopped) at once.
swarm false Turn a random subset of the enabled kinds off on each timeline.
workloadRequests true Serve workload fault requests.
clock false Install Chaos Mesh during synth up even when no clock kind is enabled yet.

The full schema is in Configuration file.

How a seed becomes a schedule

Section titled “How a seed becomes a schedule”

synth plans every fault of a timeline before the driver phase starts, from the timeline’s seed. The plan is written to plan.json and is the complete record of what the injector will do: no tool picks a target at random later.

  1. Swarm draw. synth draws this timeline’s settings: the enabled kinds (with swarm: true, each kind survives with even odds, and at least one always does), a driver parallelism between 1 and composer.maxParallel, and an intensity of 0.5, 1 or 2 times faults.intensity.
  2. Arrivals. Fault start times follow a Poisson process at that intensity, beginning about a second into the driver phase.
  3. Kind, duration and target. Each arrival picks a kind by weight, draws a duration between minDuration and maxDuration (shorter durations are more likely), picks its targets, and draws its parameters: delay and loss for netem, a CPU cap for throttle, two or three groups for a partition, an offset for a clock skip.
  4. Overlap check. An arrival that would break the overlap rules below is dropped, and the planner moves on to the next one.

Swarm testing, turning whole kinds off per timeline, tends to find more bugs than running every kind at once at low odds: some bugs need one kind of fault repeated without another kind interfering.

Overlap rules

Section titled “Overlap rules”

The planner, and the injector at run time, keep faults from piling up in ways that would only take the whole system down:

  • A disruptive fault (node.pause, node.kill, node.stop, pod.pause, pod.kill) never hits a target that another disruptive fault is already holding.
  • At most maxNodeFaults disruptive faults are active at once. A pod.kill counts as active for five seconds, while its pod restarts.
  • Only one partition per layer is active at a time: one node.partition and one pod.partition can coexist, two of the same kind cannot.
  • Two clock skips never overlap on the same pod.

Network slowdowns and CPU throttling are not disruptive, so they can overlap with anything.

At run time the injector also skips a planned fault when its target node is not running or its pod is gone, when a quiet period is active, or when it would conflict with a fault a workload requested. Skipped faults stay in the event log and the report with the reason.

What is never faulted

Section titled “What is never faulted”
  • server-0, the first k3d server. It hosts the control plane and synth’s helpers, and is where you should run your workload pod.
  • Other servers, unless faults.nodes: all.
  • Pods without the podSelector label, and pods outside the namespaces your manifests create, so two systems can share a cluster.

Pausing faults from a command

Section titled “Pausing faults from a command”

Sometimes a command needs a quiet moment, for example to measure a baseline or to let a check finish. Every container has ANTITHESIS_STOP_FAULTS set to a script that pauses fault injection, as on Antithesis:

When synth sees the request, it reverts every active fault right away and starts no new fault, planned or requested, until the time is up. Planned faults that fall in the window are skipped, not postponed. Overlapping requests extend the pause to the latest end. Without an argument the pause lasts 10 seconds. Requests are honored during the driver phase; faults are already off afterwards.

Clock faults

Section titled “Clock faults”

pod.clock shifts one pod’s clocks; pod.clock_all shifts every fault-target pod at once. Offsets range from 50 ms to 120 s, forward or back. Every skip moves CLOCK_REALTIME; half also move CLOCK_MONOTONIC, which is what timeouts computed from elapsed time read.

To use them:

  1. Add the kinds by name, since all and pod.* do not include them:

  2. Run synth up. With a clock kind enabled (or faults.clock: true), it installs Chaos Mesh 2.8.4 with helm, so helm must be on your PATH.

Caution

k3d nodes share the host’s kernel clock, so synth cannot move a node’s clock. It shifts the clocks of the target pods’ processes through Chaos Mesh. Processes started after the skip, including later kubectl exec commands, see the real time.

Clock faults work on amd64 nodes only. The released arm64 Chaos Mesh daemon crashes the processes it injects, so synth skips clock faults on arm64 nodes and records each one as skipped, with the reason. SYNTH_CLOCK_ARM64=1 forces them, for a fixed daemon image.

examples/kv/synthesis-clock.yaml is a ready-made clock configuration for the kv example.

Hand-written scenarios

Section titled “Hand-written scenarios”

When you want a specific sequence of faults instead of a random one, write it as a plan file and run it with a fresh workload:

A scenario lists faults with their start time and duration in seconds from the start of the driver phase, their targets and their parameters:

Name nodes by their full container name (k3d-<cluster>-agent-0) and pods as namespace/name. Partitions take groups instead of targets. The bundled examples/kv/scenarios has a scenario that uses every default kind and one with clock skips.

The --seed still drives everything else: the template, command seeds and the ending. Any plan.json from an earlier run works as a scenario too.

Plans are checked against the live cluster

Section titled “Plans are checked against the live cluster”

A plan that comes from a file (run --plan, replay, shrink and causality) is validated before anything is injected. Every kind must be known, every target must be a current fault-target node or pod by its exact name, and every time, duration and parameter must be a finite, non-negative number. The first bad fault stops the timeline with a harness error that names it.

Plans that synth explore draws from its corpus are different: their targets are mapped onto the current pods and nodes, and faults whose target is gone are dropped and noted in the report.
